# Knowledge workspace collaboration rules

## Goal

Help maintain a local-first knowledge system that turns evidence into decisions and actions. Prefer useful results over process theater.

## Writing boundaries

- Before creating a file, identify its knowledge area, active project, and document type.
- Search for an existing canonical document first. Update it instead of creating `final`, `latest`, `v2`, or duplicate summaries.
- Do not create business files in the workspace root or generic `output`, `tmp`, `docs`, or `assets` folders.
- Every new note must be reachable from a nearby index and must link to its parent topic or evidence.
- Do not invent sources, owners, dates, metrics, completion states, or user intent.

## Knowledge notes

Knowledge notes must include:

- creation and update dates;
- a concise change summary;
- source summary and source reliability;
- conclusion confidence;
- the human's reading status;
- body sections for sources/evidence and change history.

Reading status describes the human only: unread, reading, read, review, or unknown. AI generation and source verification are separate.

## Judgment quality

When a decision involves substantial resources, irreversible action, or meaningful strategy:

- inspect relevant existing knowledge first;
- surface only decision-changing failure paths, user counterexamples, and data gaps;
- state assumptions, confidence, next evidence, and stop conditions.

Routine formatting and low-risk maintenance do not require a separate adversarial report.

## Permissions

- Read, analyze, draft, and perform reversible in-scope edits without repeated confirmation.
- Do not publish, send messages to others, spend money, expose data, or perform irreversible actions without explicit authorization.
- Never print or store credentials in normal documents.

## Completion

Completion means the requested artifact or state exists, relevant checks passed, and problems introduced by the task were fixed. Drafted, configured, tested, and end-to-end usable are different states.

After writing, verify the changed scope, links, and any task-specific tests. Report unrelated historical problems separately.
